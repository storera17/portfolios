## *Data Preparation & Feature Creation*

The dataset used in this project contained song lyrics along with metadata, including artist names and song titles. The objective of the analysis was to identify thematic relationships between songs. This allowed the lyrical text to serve as the primary source of information for the modeling process. Before modeling, the dataset underwent preprocessing steps to prepare the data for machine learning analysis. These steps included loading the data, removing duplicate records, normalizing the lyric text, and removing observations with a minimal amount of usability.

To capture meaning from the lyric data, the lyric text was converted into numerical representations. This conversion was done using the CountVectorizer and TfidfVectorizer functions from sklearn. Furthermore, TF-IDF is then reduced to a dense lower-dimensional representation with truncated singular value decomposition. These functions vectorize a line of text using the “Bag-of-Words” approach. The “Bag-of-Words” approach counts the frequencies of words that appear. CountVectorizer, specifically, counts the raw frequency of each word in a text document and maps them to a fixed-length vector. The TfidfVectorizer measures a word’s relevance by multiplying its term frequency by its inverse document frequency. When using TF-IDF, the model can find important words or classify songs into a group by keyword frequency, like Christmas songs grouped as Christmas music.

Vectorization allows unigrams and bigrams to be captured in feature space. In other words, both individual words and short phrases were captured. The implications of the capturing of phrases and words are demonstrated in the model recognition of phrases such as “ve got” and “love love”. Without the ability to capture bigrams, the model would miss these short phrases.

## *Modeling Breakdown*

Clustering was chosen because the dataset does not contain predefined labels indicating lyrical themes. Unsupervised clustering methods allow the model to detect natural groupings within the data without requiring labeled training examples. By grouping songs according to lyrical similarity, clustering can reveal latent thematic patterns across the dataset.

The TF–IDF representation of the lyrics in order to identify groups of songs with similar linguistic characteristics. The algorithm partitions songs into clusters such that songs within the same cluster share similar word usage patterns. This approach allows the analysis to identify segments of songs that may represent distinct lyrical themes.

The MiniBatch K-Means clustering algorithm was applied in this analysis. This is a variation of the traditional K-Means clustering algorithm. The Minibatch K-means clustering algorithm is designed for large datasets and improves computational efficiency by updating cluster centroids using small random batches of data rather than the entire dataset simultaneously. These centroids are randomly selected, and each song lyric is examined and assigned according to the similarity in the TF-IDF feature space. The centroids are updated based on the assigned songs. The clusters are updated until they are stabilized.

The number of cluster selections is based on the number of clusters. The number of clusters is a critical component of unsupervised learning. The diagnostic metrics used to guide the selection process were inertia and silhouette score. Inertia measures how tightly grouped observations are within each cluster. Furthermore, lower values indicate more compact clusters. Silhouette score evaluates the similarity of an observation to its own cluster compared to other clusters. Higher silhouette scores indicate better separation between clusters. During the modeling process, alternative feature representations and clustering configurations were explored. Some configurations produced clusters that were difficult to interpret or dominated by common words rather than meaningful themes. These alternatives were rejected because they did not produce segmentation results that could realistically support business decision-making.

## *Conclusion*

This project demonstrates that lyrical text contains meaningful patterns that can be used to group songs into interpretable thematic segments. By applying natural language processing techniques and clustering algorithms to song lyrics, the analysis identified distinct groups of songs based on shared word usage patterns. The primary advantage of this approach is that it introduces an additional dimension of similarity between songs beyond traditional audio features. Lyrical analysis allows streaming platforms to capture emotional and narrative themes that influence how listeners perceive music.

However, several limitations should be considered when interpreting these results. First, clustering outcomes are sensitive to preprocessing choices such as stopword removal, tokenization, and vocabulary filtering. Changes to these steps may alter the structure of the resulting clusters. Second, TF-IDF treats words independently and does not fully capture the contextual meaning of lyrics or narrative structure within songs. Third, lyrical similarity does not necessarily reflect overall musical similarity, as songs with similar lyrical themes may differ significantly in genre, tempo, or production style. Finally, clustering algorithms are sensitive to initialization and modeling parameters, meaning that different configurations may produce different clustering results.

## *Final Executive Recommendation*

The company should consider incorporating lyrical similarity as a complementary feature within playlist generation and recommendation systems. The clustering analysis demonstrates that song lyrics can be grouped into interpretable thematic segments that may support playlist design and thematic discovery. However, lyrical segmentation should not replace existing recommendation features such as audio characteristics or listening behavior. Instead, lyrical clustering should be integrated alongside these signals to provide a more comprehensive representation of song similarity. Combining lyrical analysis with behavioral and acoustic data may improve playlist coherence, enhance recommendation accuracy, and support richer discovery experiences for listeners.
